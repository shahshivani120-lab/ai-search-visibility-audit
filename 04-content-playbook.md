## 1. The core principle

> **Write the best, clearest answer to a specific buyer question, and make that answer easy to lift out.**

AI picks **passages**, not whole pages. Every key section should make sense if it were copied on its own into an answer.

## 2. The 10 rules of citable content

### Rule 1 · One page, one main question

Each page should answer one buyer question well, like "Is Lattice good for law firms?". A page trying to cover everything ranks for nothing in particular.

### Rule 2 · Answer first

Put the direct answer in the **first 2–3 sentences**, then explain. This is sometimes called **BLUF** (Bottom Line Up Front) or the **inverted pyramid**.

❌ *"In today's fast-changing legal landscape, firms face many challenges when it comes to talent…"*
✅ *"Lattice works for some law firms, but it wasn't built for legal workflows. Firms that need partner-led reviews, billable-hour context and upward feedback usually need a legal-specific tool."*

### Rule 3 · Use the buyer's words

Use the exact phrasing buyers use in headings and opening lines. If buyers ask "Harvey vs Legora", use that phrase, and its variations ("Legora vs Harvey", "Harvey or Legora").

### Rule 4 · Be specific

Replace vague claims with facts AI can quote:

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| "Many associates leave early" | "Firm-wide associate attrition was 27% in [year], according to [source]" |
| "Affordable pricing" | "Plans start at $X per user per month, billed annually" |
| "Works with popular tools" | "Integrates with QuickBooks Online, Xero and Microsoft 365" |

### Rule 5 · Add something original

Original data, expert quotes, real examples or a unique framework. If your page says the same thing as 50 others, AI has no reason to cite **you**. If it contains a statistic that exists only on your page, AI must cite you to use it.

Ideas: customer surveys, anonymised product data, benchmark studies, analysis of public data, expert interviews, case studies with numbers.

### Rule 6 · Structure for scanning

- Descriptive H2/H3 headings (often phrased as questions)
- Paragraphs of 2–4 sentences
- Bullet lists for criteria, steps and features
- Tables for comparisons and numbers
- A short summary or "key takeaways" box near the top

### Rule 7 · Be fair to competitors

Balanced comparisons are trusted more. Say where a competitor is genuinely strong. A page that says "[Competitor] is great for X; [Brand] is better for Y" is far more quotable than one that says "[Brand] is best at everything".

### Rule 8 · Show who wrote it and when

- Author name, role and a short bio showing relevant experience
- "Published" and "Last updated" dates
- Sources linked for every statistic

### Rule 9 · Keep it in plain HTML

Key text should be in the page's HTML, not only in images, videos, PDFs, accordions loaded by JavaScript or interactive widgets. If the answer is in a chart, also write it in a sentence.

### Rule 10 · Keep it fresh

Review key pages every 3–6 months. Update numbers, pricing, product names and the "last updated" date. AI tools that search the web tend to prefer current information.

## 3. Content formats that get cited

| Format | Buyer stage | Example title | Why AI cites it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Comparison (X vs Y)** | Comparison | "Legora vs Harvey AI: What US Law Firms Should Know" | Directly answers "X vs Y" prompts |
| **Is X good for Y?** | Comparison | "Is Lattice Good for Law Firms?" | Matches a very common question pattern |
| **Best X for Y (buyer guide)** | Consideration | "Best Performance Management Software for Law Firms" | Gives AI a ready-made shortlist with criteria |
| **Alternatives** | Consideration | "7 Lattice Alternatives for Law Firms" | Answers "alternatives to X" prompts |
| **How to choose** | Consideration | "How to Choose 360 Feedback Software: 9 Questions" | Supplies criteria AI repeats |
| **Original research** | Awareness | "Why US Law Firm Associates Leave in the First 4 Years" | Unique data that must be attributed |
| **Complete guide** | Awareness | "Attorney Performance Review: Complete Guide" | Comprehensive reference on a topic |
| **Glossary / definition** | Awareness | "What Is an Upward Review?" | Answers "what is X" prompts |
| **Pricing explainer** | Decision | "How Much Does Restaurant POS Cost in Australia?" | Answers cost questions with numbers |
| **Trends** | Awareness | "7 Legal Trends Reshaping Law Firm Management" | Timely, list-based, easy to quote |

## 4. Template: comparison page

```markdown
# [Brand A] vs [Brand B]: [Which Is Better for Audience]? ([Year])

**Last updated:** [date] · **By:** [Author, role]

## Quick answer
[2–3 sentences: who should choose A, who should choose B, and the main difference.]

## At a glance
| | [Brand A] | [Brand B] |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | | |
| Starting price | | |
| Key strength | | |
| Main limitation | | |
| Integrations | | |
| Setup time | | |

## What is [Brand A]?
[2–3 sentences, factual.]

## What is [Brand B]?
[2–3 sentences, factual.]

## [Brand A] vs [Brand B]: key differences
### 1. [Difference, e.g. Pricing]
### 2. [Difference, e.g. Features for audience]
### 3. [Difference, e.g. Ease of use]
### 4. [Difference, e.g. Support and security]

## When to choose [Brand A]
- [Situation]

## When to choose [Brand B]
- [Situation]
- [Situation]

## Frequently asked questions
### Is [Brand A] cheaper than [Brand B]?
### Can I switch from [Brand B] to [Brand A]?

## Methodology
[How you compared: sources, dates, hands-on testing.]
```

## 5. Template: "Is X good for Y?" page

```markdown
# Is [Product] Good for [Audience]? An Honest Review ([Year])

## Short answer
[Yes / No / It depends, in one sentence. Then 1–2 sentences on why.]

## What [audience] need from [category]
- [Need 1]
- [Need 2]
- [Need 3]

## Where [Product] works well for [audience]
## Where [Product] falls short for [audience]
## What [audience] use instead
| Tool | Best for | Why |
|---|---|---|

## Verdict
## FAQs
```

## 6. Template: buyer guide

```markdown
# Best [Category] for [Audience] in [Year]

## Quick picks
- **Best overall:** [Tool] – [one-line reason]
- **Best for small [audience]:** [Tool] – [reason]
- **Best for [specific need]:** [Tool] – [reason]

## How we evaluated
[Criteria list and method. This builds trust and gives AI quotable criteria.]

## What to look for in [category]
1. [Criterion and why it matters]
2. ...

## The [n] best [category] tools for [audience]
### 1. [Tool]
- Best for:
- Key features:
- Pricing:
- Pros:
- Cons:

## Comparison table
## FAQs
```

**Note on fairness:** if your own product is on the list, say so clearly ("Disclosure: we make [Brand]"). Transparency builds trust with readers and AI alike.

## 7. Template: original research page

```markdown
# [Finding as a headline, e.g. "Why US Law Firm Associates Leave in the First 4 Years"]

## Key findings
- **[Number]%** of [group] [finding]
- **[Number]** [finding]
- [Finding]

## About this research
[Who, how many, when, method. Be precise: AI and journalists need this to cite you.]

## Finding 1: [headline]
[Explanation + chart + the same number written in text]

## Finding 2: [headline]
## What this means for [audience]
## How to cite this research
"[Title]", [Brand], [Month Year], [URL]
```

Research pages also earn backlinks and press mentions, which strengthen every other page.

## 8. Template: FAQ section

```markdown
## Frequently asked questions

### [Real question buyers ask]?
[Direct answer in the first sentence. 2–4 sentences total.]

### [Next question]?
[Answer.]
```

Rules:

- Use **real** questions (from sales calls, support tickets, "People also ask", Reddit).
- Answer in the first sentence, without repeating the question.
- 4–8 questions per page is plenty.
- Add FAQPage schema (see `faq-schema-example.json`).
- The visible FAQ text and the schema text must match.

## 9. Before and after examples

### Example A: opening paragraph

**Before** ❌
> Choosing the right technology partner is one of the most important decisions a modern restaurant can make. With so many options available, it can be overwhelming…

**After** ✅
> A restaurant POS system in Australia typically costs $X–$Y per month for software, plus $X–$Y upfront for hardware. Kiosk ordering and online ordering integrations usually cost extra. Here's a breakdown by venue size.

### Example B: feature claim

**Before** ❌
> Our powerful platform seamlessly connects with all your favourite tools.

**After** ✅
> [Brand] integrates with Uber Eats, DoorDash and Menulog, so delivery orders go straight to the kitchen screen without re-typing.

### Example C: heading

**Before** ❌ `## Overview`
**After** ✅ `## How do law firms run upward reviews?`

## 10. Updating existing pages

Often the fastest wins come from improving pages that already rank or get cited.

1. **Find candidates:** pages with high impressions but low clicks in Search Console, or pages already cited in Bing AI Performance.
2. **Rewrite the opening** to answer the main question in 2–3 sentences.
3. **Rename vague headings** into question or statement headings.
4. **Add a table** summarising key facts.
5. **Add an FAQ section** with schema.
6. **Add specifics:** numbers, dates, examples, sources.
7. **Update the date** and any outdated facts.
8. **Request re-indexing** in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools (or ping via IndexNow).

## 11. Pre-publish checklist

- ☐ The page answers one clear buyer question
- ☐ The answer is in the first 2–3 sentences
- ☐ Title and H1 use the buyer's phrasing
- ☐ Headings are descriptive (many phrased as questions)
- ☐ Paragraphs are 2–4 sentences
- ☐ At least one table or list summarises key facts
- ☐ Contains specific numbers, names or dates
- ☐ Includes something original (data, example, expert view)
- ☐ Competitors are described fairly
- ☐ Author, published date and last-updated date are visible
- ☐ Sources are linked
- ☐ FAQ section added, with matching FAQPage schema
- ☐ Key text is in HTML (not only images or PDFs)
- ☐ Internal links from related pages point to it
- ☐ Submitted for indexing in Google and Bing
